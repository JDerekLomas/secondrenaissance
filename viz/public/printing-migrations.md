# The Spread of Printing: Printer Migrations 1450-1500

## The Birth and Dispersion of Printing

The story of how printing spread across Europe is fundamentally a story of **human migration**. When Johannes Gutenberg invented movable type printing in Mainz around 1450, he created not just a technology but a trade secret. The precise combination of metal alloys and casting processes remained proprietary knowledge for almost a century—the first "blueprint" manual wasn't published until 1540.

This meant printing could only spread one way: through the movement of trained craftsmen.

### The Sack of Mainz (1462)

The pivotal event that scattered printing across Europe was the **Sack of Mainz** in 1462, when troops of the Archbishop of Nassau attacked the city. This prompted the departure of workers from Fust and Schöffer's workshop (Gutenberg's former partners), sending trained printers fleeing to cities across Europe.

### The Lyon Phenomenon

**Lyon received 23 printers**—more than any other city. Why?

1. **Trade Fairs**: Charles VII granted Lyon tax-free fairs, making it France's economic counting house
2. **Italian Merchants**: Brought capital and international connections
3. **Geographic Position**: Gateway between France, Italy, and Germany
4. **Silk Trade**: The city was becoming a hub for luxury goods
5. **Less Guild Control**: More opportunity than established Paris

The Paris-Lyon corridor was the busiest migration route, with printers moving both directions based on opportunity.

### Venice: The Export Center

**Venice sent 14 printers to other cities** while receiving 12—it was a net exporter of printing talent. By 1480, Venice had become the printing capital of the world, producing 13% of all European books.

Why Venice dominated:
- Biggest, richest city in Europe
- Major seaport with easy paper imports
- Abundant capital from merchants
- Skilled labor force (dyers, metalworkers)
- Less church/court control than elsewhere

Printers trained in Venice's competitive market were highly sought after across Europe.

## Key Printers and Their Journeys

### William Caxton (Cologne → Westminster, 1476)
The first English printer learned his craft in **Cologne** while working as a merchant. In 1475 he printed the first book in English (in Bruges), then established England's first press at **Westminster** in 1476 near the court. He printed nearly 100 volumes including Chaucer's Canterbury Tales.

### Erhard Ratdolt (Venice → Augsburg, 1485)
A German goldsmith who set up shop in **Venice** in 1476, Ratdolt pioneered scientific illustration—his 1482 *Poeticon astronomicon* was the first printed star atlas. He invented decorative borders, title pages, and color printing. In 1486 he returned to **Augsburg** at the bishop's invitation, introducing Italian typography to Germany.

### Nicolas Jenson (Mainz → Venice, 1470)
A French engraver sent by King Charles VII to learn printing in Mainz in 1458. By 1470 he was in **Venice**, where he created the Roman typeface that remains the basis of all book typography today. At his peak he ran 12 presses simultaneously and printed 40% of all Venetian editions.

### Aldus Manutius (Rome/Ferrara → Venice, 1490)
A humanist scholar who moved to **Venice** at age 40 to found the Aldine Press. His innovations:
- Invented **italic type** (1501)
- Created the **pocket-sized book** format
- Standardized punctuation (comma, semicolon)
- First systematic publisher of Greek classics

His dolphin-and-anchor logo is still used by Doubleday Books.

### Anton Koberger (Nuremberg)
Godfather of Albrecht Dürer, Koberger built the largest publishing operation in Germany with **24 presses** and 100 workers. His 1493 *Nuremberg Chronicle*, with 1,809 woodcuts, was the most ambitious illustrated book of the era.

## The Economics of Migration

Why did printers move?

1. **Seeking Capital**: Printing required significant investment in type, presses, and paper
2. **Following Markets**: Universities, courts, and merchant cities offered customers
3. **Avoiding Competition**: Established cities had many printers; new markets offered opportunity
4. **Political Disruption**: Wars and conflicts (like the Sack of Mainz) forced relocations
5. **Patron Invitation**: Bishops and rulers invited printers to their cities

## Timeline of the Spread

- **1450s**: Gutenberg's workshop in Mainz
- **1462**: Sack of Mainz scatters printers
- **1465**: First press in Italy (Subiaco monastery, by two Germans)
- **1469**: Printing reaches Venice
- **1470**: Nicolas Jenson perfects Roman type in Venice
- **1470**: Anton Koberger opens first Nuremberg press
- **1472**: First press in Lyon (Bartholomew Buyer)
- **1476**: William Caxton brings printing to England
- **1480**: 110 European cities have presses
- **1480**: Venice becomes world printing capital
- **1490**: Aldus Manutius founds Aldine Press
- **1500**: 236 European cities have presses; 20 million books printed
